Spectral analysis and filtering#

The cultivars.spectral package reads time series and fitted systems by frequency. It answers four questions: where in the cycle a series’ variance sits (nonparametric spectra), what a fitted VAR claims at each frequency (model-implied density, coherence, phase, Geweke causality), how to separate trend from cycle (HP, Butterworth, Hamilton, Beveridge-Nelson, Baxter-King, Christiano-Fitzgerald) and how to date and compare cycles (turning points, concordance), with wavelets adding a date axis. It does not estimate models: the model-implied views take a fitted result from Vector autoregressions or Beyond the basic VAR, and long-memory estimators that read the spectrum near zero are on Diagnostics and specification testing.

Units and conventions#

Every object on this page uses the same units, so a data estimate and a model density overlay without conversion.

Frequency

\(\omega\) in radians per observation on \([0, \pi]\). \(\pi\) is the Nyquist frequency, a cycle of two observations. Results store the grid as frequencies.

Period

\(2\pi / \omega\) in observations per cycle. periods() on SpectrumEstimate and SpectralDensityResult returns it, with inf at \(\omega = 0\). The package never converts to years: for quarterly data a period of 8 is two years, for monthly data it is eight months.

Bands

Stated in periods, never in frequencies: low is the shortest period passed and high the longest. The Burns-Mitchell business cycle is 6 to 32 at quarterly frequency (\(\omega\) from \(2\pi/32 = 0.196\) to \(2\pi/6 = 1.047\)) and 18 to 96 at monthly frequency. Periods below 2 do not exist at the sampling frequency.

Normalization

Densities carry the \(1/2\pi\) factor: \(\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} f(\omega)\,d\omega = \gamma(0)\), so twice the integral of a spectrum over \([0, \pi]\) is the variance, and white noise of variance \(\sigma^2\) has the flat spectrum \(\sigma^2 / 2\pi\).

Matrix axes

Spectral matrices are (n_frequencies, k, k); series are addressed by name ("y1", "y2", … unless names is given), not by index.

Nonparametric spectra from data#

The three estimators smooth the periodogram \(I(\omega_j) = (2\pi N)^{-1} |\sum_t y_t e^{-i\omega_j t}|^2\), which is inconsistent on its own, and each ordinate of the result is approximately \(f(\omega)\chi^2_\nu/\nu\). They take the panel at construction and estimate through compute().

All three take detrend ("n", "c" the default, or "ct") and names, need at least 32 observations, and return a SpectrumEstimate. Multitaper is the default choice: least leakage at a given variance, full Fourier grid. A three-variable system whose first series has an AR(2) cycle of period 12:

import numpy as np
from cultivars.spectral.periodogram import (
    DaniellSpectrum, MultitaperSpectrum, WelchSpectrum,
)

rng = np.random.default_rng(1)
n = 400
y = np.zeros((n, 3))
for t in range(2, n):
    y[t, 0] = 1.3 * y[t - 1, 0] - 0.6 * y[t - 2, 0] + rng.standard_normal()
    y[t, 1] = 0.6 * y[t - 1, 0] + 0.3 * y[t - 1, 1] + rng.standard_normal()
    y[t, 2] = 0.5 * y[t - 1, 1] + 0.3 * y[t - 1, 2] + rng.standard_normal()
names = ("cycle", "output", "prices")

est = MultitaperSpectrum(y, names=names).compute()
print(est.method, est.frequencies.shape, est.density.shape)
print(est.degrees_of_freedom, round(est.bandwidth, 4))
power = est.spectrum("cycle")
peak = 1 + int(np.argmax(power[1:]))
print(round(float(est.frequencies[peak]), 3), round(float(est.periods()[peak]), 1))
print(round(float(2 * np.trapezoid(power, est.frequencies) / y[:, 0].var()), 2))

The peak sits at period 11.8 against the AR(2)’s theoretical 12.0, and twice the integral recovers the sample variance. The three estimators trade resolution for stability differently on the same data:

for e in (est, DaniellSpectrum(y, names=names).compute(),
          WelchSpectrum(y, names=names).compute()):
    print(e.method, len(e.frequencies), round(e.degrees_of_freedom, 1),
          round(e.bandwidth, 4))

daniell 201 41.0 0.1571
welch 26 28.4 0.1257

SpectrumEstimate provides spectrum(name), coherence(first, second) (squared, biased upward by about \(2/\nu\) under zero coherence), confidence_interval(name, alpha=0.05) (pointwise, constant ratio, so plot spectra on a log scale), periods() and summary().

Model-implied spectra from fitted systems#

SpectralDensity evaluates the closed form

\[f(\omega) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\, \Psi(\omega)\, \Sigma_u\, \Psi(\omega)^\ast, \qquad \Psi(\omega) = \Bigl[I - \sum_{l=1}^{p} A_l e^{-i\omega l}\Bigr]^{-1},\]

of a fitted result on an inclusive grid of n_frequencies points (default 256) on \([0, \pi]\) (Hamilton 1994, ch. 6). It re-estimates nothing and reads no data: the input is the result’s coefficients and sigma_u. The result must satisfy the closed-system protocol (names, nobs, sigma_u, resid, coefficients, k_endog, ma_representation), which VAR and VECM results do; anything else raises SpecificationError at construction.

The model’s grid matches a periodogram’s Fourier grid when the sample length \(N\) is even and n_frequencies = N // 2 + 1, so the two overlay directly:

from cultivars.multivariate.reduced_form.vector_autoregression import VAR
from cultivars.spectral.density import SpectralDensity

fit = VAR(y, order=2, names=names).fit()
model = SpectralDensity(fit, n_frequencies=201).compute()
print(bool(np.allclose(model.frequencies, est.frequencies)))
lower, upper = est.confidence_interval("cycle")
implied = model.spectrum("cycle")
print(round(float(np.mean((lower <= implied) & (implied <= upper))), 2))

True
0.97

The VAR(2) density lies inside the data’s 95% band at 97% of the ordinates. Where the two disagree is where the model is misspecified, frequency by frequency.

w = model.frequencies
business = (w >= 2 * np.pi / 32) & (w <= 2 * np.pi / 6)

Spectral Granger causality#

Geweke (1982) splits the effect’s spectrum into an intrinsic part and a part driven by the cause’s innovations:

\[f_{x \to y}(\omega) = \ln \frac{S_{yy}(\omega)} {H_{yy}(\omega)\, \tilde\Sigma_{yy}\, \overline{H_{yy}(\omega)}} \;\ge\; 0, \qquad \frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^{\pi} f_{x \to y}(\omega)\,d\omega = \ln \frac{\sigma^2_{\text{restricted}}}{\sigma^2_{\text{full}}}.\]

SpectralCausality computes the unconditional measure for every ordered pair from one fitted system. For more than two variables each pair’s marginal process is factored by Wilson’s (1972) algorithm, following Dhamala, Rangarajan and Ding (2008); nothing is re-estimated.

from cultivars.spectral.causality import (
    ConditionalSpectralCausality, SpectralCausality,
)

geweke = SpectralCausality(fit, n_frequencies=201).compute()
print(geweke.measure.shape)
print(geweke.integrated().round(3))
print(round(geweke.band("cycle", "output", low_period=6, high_period=32), 3),
      round(geweke.band("output", "cycle", low_period=6, high_period=32), 3))

(201, 3, 3)
[[0.    0.005 0.003]
 [0.628 0.    0.007]
 [0.285 0.485 0.   ]]
1.569 0.001

The axes of measure and integrated() are [effect, cause]: row 1, column 0 above is cycle -> output. pair(cause, effect) and band(cause, effect, ...) take the spoken order and transpose for you.

The unconditional measure credits cycle with causing prices (0.285), although the effect runs entirely through output. Geweke’s (1984) conditional measure removes that, but needs a second, restricted model fitted without the cause, which you supply:

restricted = VAR(y[:, 1:], order=6, names=names[1:]).fit()
conditional = ConditionalSpectralCausality(
    fit, restricted, n_frequencies=201,
).compute()
print(conditional.cause, conditional.effect_names, conditional.measure.shape)
print(conditional.integrated().round(3).tolist(), round(conditional.consistency, 3))

cycle ('output', 'prices') (201, 2)
[0.615, 0.004] 0.255

The cause is inferred as the one name the restricted model lacks; the remaining names must appear in the same order. consistency is the largest relative gap between the two models’ implied innovation variances: zero in population, and large when the restricted model is too short (the true restricted process is generally VARMA, hence the generous order=6).

Trend-cycle filters#

The four classes in filters define the trend four ways and return one record, DecompositionFilterResult, with trend and cycle of shape (rows, k). Each filter is applied column by column; a 1-D series becomes one column.

Class (defaults)

Trend is

Bill on the result

HodrickPrescottFilter(penalty=1600.0) (Hodrick and Prescott 1997)

Minimizer of \(\sum(y_t-\tau_t)^2 + \lambda\sum(\Delta^2\tau_t)^2\)

provisional: the first and last \(\lceil\text{cutoff}\rceil\) rows (cutoff 39.7 at 1600) will be revised

ButterworthFilter(period=32.0, order=2) (Gomez 2001)

Same family, stated by cutoff period and order

provisional as for HP; penalty reports the implied \(\lambda\)

HamiltonFilter(horizon=8, lags=4) (Hamilton 2018)

Fitted value of \(y_{t+h}\) on a constant and \(y_t,\dots,y_{t-p+1}\)

offset \(= h + p - 1\) rows lost at the start

BeveridgeNelsonDecomposition(result) (Beveridge and Nelson 1981; Morley 2002)

Long-horizon forecast net of drift, from a fitted ARIMA(p, 1, q)

offset 1; conditional on the ARIMA

filter(data) applies the first three; the Beveridge-Nelson decomposition takes a fitted ARIMA result with d = 1, no seasonal part, no exogenous regressors and trend "n" or "c", and runs through compute(). Row i of the output is input observation offset + i.

A quarterly log-level with drift, a random-walk component and a period-20 cycle:

from cultivars.spectral.filters import (
    BeveridgeNelsonDecomposition, ButterworthFilter, HamiltonFilter,
    HodrickPrescottFilter,
)
from cultivars.univariate.box_jenkins import ARIMA

rng = np.random.default_rng(4)
n = 240
t = np.arange(n)
true_cycle = 0.02 * np.sin(2 * np.pi * t / 20)
log_gdp = 0.005 * t + np.cumsum(0.002 * rng.standard_normal(n)) + true_cycle

hp = HodrickPrescottFilter().filter(log_gdp)
print(hp.trend.shape, hp.offset, round(hp.cutoff, 1), int(hp.provisional.sum()))
bw = ButterworthFilter(period=32, order=2).filter(log_gdp)
print(round(bw.penalty, 1), int(bw.provisional.sum()))
ham = HamiltonFilter().filter(log_gdp)
print(ham.offset, ham.cycle.shape, bool(ham.provisional.any()))

def fit_to_truth(res):
    c = res.cycle[:, 0]
    aligned = true_cycle[res.offset:res.offset + len(c)]
    return round(float(np.corrcoef(c, aligned)[0, 1]), 2)

print(fit_to_truth(hp), fit_to_truth(bw), fit_to_truth(ham))

(240, 1) 0 39.7 80
677.1 64
11 (229, 1) False
0.97 0.97 0.66

Butterworth at period=32, order=2 is HP at \(\lambda = 677\). The Hamilton cycle is the eight-quarter forecast error, so it carries the random-walk component’s eight-step variation as well as the cycle; that is its definition, not a failure. The point of Hamilton (2018) shows on a pure random walk, which has no cycle: HP manufactures one, about as large as the increments themselves, and the summary prints that ratio:

walk = np.cumsum(rng.standard_normal(n))
hp_walk = HodrickPrescottFilter().filter(walk)
print(round(float(hp_walk.cycle.std() / np.diff(walk).std()), 2))

bn = BeveridgeNelsonDecomposition(ARIMA(log_gdp, order=(1, 1, 0)).fit()).compute()
print(bn.method, bn.offset, bn.cycle.shape, bn.order)

Band-pass filters#

The ideal band-pass between periods low and high has weights \(b_0 = (\omega_h - \omega_l)/\pi\), \(b_j = (\sin j\omega_h - \sin j\omega_l)/(\pi j)\), with \(\omega_l = 2\pi/\text{high}\) and \(\omega_h = 2\pi/\text{low}\). Both filters approximate it and return BandPassResult with cycle, trend (everything outside the band, low and high frequencies), low, high, method, offset and symmetric.

BaxterKingFilter(low=6.0, high=32.0, truncation=12)

Baxter and King (1999): symmetric weights truncated at lag \(K\) and recentred to sum to zero. Phase-neutral and final everywhere it is defined; loses truncation rows at each end.

ChristianoFitzgeraldFilter(low=6.0, high=32.0)

Christiano and Fitzgerald (2003), random-walk version: keeps every row by letting the weights turn asymmetric near the ends. symmetric marks the rows with at least ceil(high) observations on both sides; elsewhere the value is phase-shifted and will be revised.

from cultivars.spectral.band_pass import (
    BaxterKingFilter, ChristianoFitzgeraldFilter,
)

bk = BaxterKingFilter().filter(log_gdp)
print(bk.cycle.shape, bk.offset, bk.low, bk.high, bool(bk.symmetric.all()))
print(fit_to_truth(bk))
cf = ChristianoFitzgeraldFilter().filter(log_gdp)
print(cf.cycle.shape, cf.offset, int(cf.symmetric.sum()))

(216, 1) 12 6.0 32.0 True
0.99
(240, 1) 0 176

See Nuances and gotchas before relying on the Christiano-Fitzgerald output.

Dating cycles#

TurningPoints applies the Bry and Boschan (1971) rules in Harding and Pagan’s (2002) form: a peak is a local maximum over window observations on each side, turns alternate, and phases and cycles must last at least min_phase and min_cycle observations. convention="quarterly" is \((2, 2, 5)\) and "monthly" is \((5, 6, 15)\); each element can be overridden by keyword. date(endog) returns a TurningPointsResult with peaks, troughs (indices into the input), a contraction indicator, a phases table of (start, end, is_contraction, duration, amplitude, excess) and n_cycles. The concordance index of Harding and Pagan (2006) is the share of dates on which two contraction indicators agree. Dating the Baxter-King cycle above against the true cycle’s chronology on the same rows:

from cultivars.spectral.cycles import TurningPoints, concordance

dated = TurningPoints().date(bk.cycle[:, 0])
print(dated.peaks.tolist(), dated.n_cycles)
rows = slice(bk.offset, bk.offset + bk.cycle.shape[0])
truth = TurningPoints().date(true_cycle[rows])
print(truth.peaks.tolist())
print(round(dated.concordance(truth), 2),
      round(concordance(dated, truth.contraction), 2))
print(dated.phases[0][:4])

[13, 32, 53, 73, 93, 114, 133, 153, 173, 193, 212] 10
[13, 33, 53, 73, 93, 113, 133, 153, 173, 193, 213]
0.96 0.96
(3, 13, False, 10)

Indices are positions in the series passed to date: add bk.offset to map a Baxter-King row back to the original sample. concordance also accepts a probability series (thresholded at one half), such as smoothed recession probabilities from MarkovSwitchingARResult.

Wavelets#

MODWT (Percival and Walden 2000) splits one series into octave bands, each aligned in time: \(y_t = \sum_{j=1}^J D_{j,t} + S_{J,t}\), where level \(j\) covers periods \([2^j, 2^{j+1})\). wavelet is "la8" (default), "d4" or "haar"; levels defaults to the deepest level that keeps a boundary-free coefficient. transform(endog) takes a 1-D series of at least 32 observations.

WaveletCoherence (Torrence and Compo 1998; Grinsted, Moore and Jevrejeva 2004) computes Morlet coherence over scale and date, with a cone of influence and an AR(1) surrogate significance level. compute(first, second) takes two aligned 1-D series. The surrogates dominate the cost: the default surrogates=300 takes seconds; surrogates=0 skips the significance level. Pass seed for a reproducible level.

A period-24 cycle shared by two series in the first half of the sample only, the second series lagging by three observations:

from cultivars.spectral.wavelets import MODWT, WaveletCoherence

rng = np.random.default_rng(5)
n = 256
t = np.arange(n)
x = np.sin(2 * np.pi * t / 24) + 0.5 * rng.standard_normal(n)
shared = np.where(t < 128, np.sin(2 * np.pi * (t - 3) / 24), 0.0)
z = shared + 0.5 * rng.standard_normal(n)

mra = MODWT().transform(x)
print(mra.levels, mra.wavelet, mra.details.shape, mra.periods[3])
print(bool(np.allclose(mra.details.sum(axis=0) + mra.smooth, x)))
print(int(np.argmax(mra.variance)) + 1, mra.variance.round(3).tolist())

wc = WaveletCoherence(surrogates=50, seed=0).compute(x, z)
print(wc.coherence.shape, wc.periods.shape)
band = (wc.periods >= 20) & (wc.periods <= 28)
significant = wc.significant()
print(round(float(significant[band][:, 40:100].mean()), 2),
      round(float(significant[band][:, 160:220].mean()), 2))
print(round(wc.lead((20, 28)), 1))

MODWTResult also gives coefficients, scaling, degrees_of_freedom, variance_interval(alpha), scales and summary(); WaveletCoherenceResult gives phase, scales, cone (True inside the cone of influence), significance and summary().

How the pieces connect#

  • Fitted VARs feed the model-implied views. SpectralDensity, SpectralCausality and ConditionalSpectralCausality accept any result that satisfies the closed-system protocol, the same protocol the identification schemes on Structural identification read. The time-domain counterpart of the integrated Geweke measure is granger_causality(cause, effect) on a fitted VAR result (Vector autoregressions).

  • Data and model overlay. Fit a VAR, estimate a multitaper spectrum on the same sample with n_frequencies = N // 2 + 1, and read the model density against the data’s confidence band, as in Model-implied spectra from fitted systems. This is a frequency-wise misspecification check to set beside the residual diagnostics on Diagnostics and specification testing.

  • Filters feed dating. A band-pass or trend-cycle cycle column is the input for a growth-cycle chronology; the level itself is the input for a classical chronology.

  • Models and filters agree where they should. The smooth-trend UnobservedComponents model reproduces the HP trend at the penalty \(\sigma^2_\varepsilon / \sigma^2_\zeta\) it estimates (Univariate linear models); its cycle period is \(2\pi/\lambda_c\) in the units of this page.

  • Regime models feed concordance. Smoothed recession probabilities from a Markov-switching model (Nonlinear and regime-switching univariate models) go straight into concordance().

Nuances and gotchas#

  • Christiano-Fitzgerald output is wrong in this release. The endpoint weights of ChristianoFitzgeraldFilter are off by one lag, so the weights applied at each date do not sum to zero: a constant series returns a nonzero cycle, and a trending level leaks into the cycle everywhere, not only near the ends. Above, the CF cycle of log_gdp correlates 0.45 with the true cycle where Baxter-King reaches 0.99. Use BaxterKingFilter until this is fixed, and check any CF output on a constant first:

    flat = np.ones(100)
cf_flat = ChristianoFitzgeraldFilter().filter(flat).cycle
bk_flat = BaxterKingFilter().filter(flat).cycle
print(round(float(np.abs(cf_flat).max()), 3),
      round(float(np.abs(bk_flat).max()), 3), fit_to_truth(cf))

    0.215 0.0 0.45

  • VARMA results give the wrong density. SpectralDensity and SpectralCausality build \(\Psi(\omega)\) from coefficients alone. For a VARMA result that is the autoregressive block only, so the moving-average part is silently dropped (a VARMA(1, 1) with MA coefficient 0.8 returns about half the true variance). Pass VAR or VECM results.

  • Not every multivariate result is accepted. BVAR results lack ma_representation and are refused with SpecificationError.

  • Unit roots. A near-unit root gives a legitimately huge density at \(\omega = 0\) (a fitted VECM in levels does this); an exact unit root makes the lag polynomial singular and compute() raises NumericalError. Summaries exclude \(\omega = 0\) from the peak search for this reason.

  • Grids align only by construction. A periodogram’s grid is \(2\pi j/N\), \(j = 0,\dots,\lfloor N/2 \rfloor\), ending short of \(\pi\) when \(N\) is odd; the model grid always ends at \(\pi\). Welch uses the segment’s grid, segment // 2 + 1 points. Check np.allclose on the two frequencies arrays before comparing ordinates.

  • Phase signs differ between modules. SpectralDensityResult.phase is negative when cause leads effect. WaveletCoherenceResult phase and lead are positive when the first series leads.

  • Axis order of causality arrays is [effect, cause]. measure[:, i, j] is j -> i; use pair(cause, effect) to avoid transposition errors. band raises if no grid point falls in the band; refine n_frequencies for narrow bands.

  • Conditional causality needs matching names. The restricted model must contain the full model’s names minus exactly one, in the same order; give it explicit names. Nothing checks that the two models were fitted on the same sample; consistency will be large if they were not.

  • Rows lost and rows provisional. Hamilton loses horizon + lags - 1 rows at the start, Baxter-King truncation at each end, Beveridge-Nelson one. HP and Butterworth keep every row but flag ceil(cutoff) rows at each edge as provisional (80 of 240 at penalty=1600); a sample shorter than twice the cutoff is provisional throughout.

  • The band-pass “trend” is not a trend. BandPassResult.trend is the input minus the cycle, so it holds the frequencies above the band as well as below it. Its summary’s “cycle share of variance” uses that trend, so for a trending level it is close to zero; filter a detrended or differenced series to read it as a variance share.

  • Turning points near the ends. The first and last window observations can never be turns, and a phase open at the sample end is absent from phases. The quarterly triple dates spurious short phases on noisy series; use convention="monthly", a larger min_phase, or a smoothed input.

  • Concordance base rate. Two independent chronologies with contraction shares \(p, p'\) agree at rate \(pp' + (1-p)(1-p')\), not one half. A probability of exactly 0.5 counts as expansion.

  • Wavelet edges. MODWT is circular: the first \((2^j - 1)(L - 1)\) coefficients at level \(j\) wrap around, and the variance excludes them, so the deepest level has very few degrees of freedom (degrees_of_freedom shows it). Coherence inside cone is contaminated by padding; significant() and lead ignore it, and significant() raises if surrogates=0.

  • Univariate inputs. MODWT, TurningPoints and WaveletCoherence take 1-D series; the periodogram estimators and the filters take panels. SpectralCausality needs at least two variables.
