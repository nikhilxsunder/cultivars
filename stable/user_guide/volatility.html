Volatility models#

This page covers models for the time-varying variance of a single series. The observation-driven family in cultivars.univariate.conditional_variance (GARCH, GJR, EGARCH, FIGARCH, with constant or ARMA means) makes the variance a function of past shocks; the latent family in cultivars.univariate.stochastic_volatility (SV, UCSV) makes it a state with its own innovation. They answer “how volatile is the series now, how persistent are variance shocks, and is there a leverage effect?”. Constant-variance mean models are on Univariate linear models; volatility in a system of series (BVAR with stochastic volatility, factor SV, identification through heteroskedasticity) is on Bayesian VARs, Large and high-dimensional systems and Structural identification.

GARCH-type models#

Every model in conditional_variance writes

\[y_t = \mu_t + \varepsilon_t, \qquad \varepsilon_t = \sigma_t z_t, \qquad z_t \sim N(0, 1),\]

with \(\mu_t\) zero, a constant, or an AR/ARMA recursion, and \(\sigma_t^2\) one of the recursions of Engle (1982), Bollerslev (1986), Glosten, Jagannathan and Runkle (1993), Nelson (1991) or Baillie, Bollerslev and Mikkelsen (1996). Mean and variance are estimated jointly by maximizing the Gaussian likelihood; the mean uses conditional sum of squares, and the variance recursion starts from an exponentially weighted backcast. fit() takes no arguments.

import numpy as np
from cultivars.univariate.conditional_variance import GARCH

rng = np.random.default_rng(1)
n = 2000
h, r = np.ones(n), np.zeros(n)
for t in range(1, n):
    h[t] = 0.05 + 0.08 * (r[t - 1] - 0.05) ** 2 + 0.9 * h[t - 1]
    r[t] = 0.05 + np.sqrt(h[t]) * rng.standard_normal()

garch = GARCH(r).fit()
print(garch.vol, garch.order, garch.nobs, garch.n_params)
print({k: round(v, 3) for k, v in garch.params.items()})
print(round(garch.persistence, 3), round(garch.half_life, 1),
      round(garch.unconditional_variance, 3))

Asymmetry: GJR and EGARCH#

\[ \begin{align}\begin{aligned}\text{GJR:}\quad \sigma_t^2 = \omega + \sum_i \alpha_i \varepsilon_{t-i}^2 + \sum_k \gamma_k \varepsilon_{t-k}^2\,1\{\varepsilon_{t-k} < 0\} + \sum_j \beta_j \sigma_{t-j}^2,\\\text{EGARCH:}\quad \log\sigma_t^2 = \omega + \sum_i \alpha_i\bigl(|z_{t-i}| - \sqrt{2/\pi}\bigr) + \sum_k \gamma_k z_{t-k} + \sum_j \beta_j \log\sigma_{t-j}^2.\end{aligned}\end{align} \]

The two use opposite sign conventions for leverage: in GJR, \(\gamma > 0\) means bad news raises volatility more; in EGARCH the same effect is \(\gamma < 0\). GJR’s \(\gamma\) is searched unconstrained, so a negative estimate is reported as reverse asymmetry.

from cultivars.univariate.conditional_variance import EGARCH, GJR

rng = np.random.default_rng(0)
h, lev = np.ones(2500), np.zeros(2500)
for t in range(1, 2500):
    h[t] = 0.02 + 0.05 * lev[t - 1] ** 2 + 0.85 * h[t - 1]
    h[t] += 0.10 * lev[t - 1] ** 2 * (lev[t - 1] < 0)
    lev[t] = np.sqrt(h[t]) * rng.standard_normal()

fits = [GARCH(lev).fit(), GJR(lev).fit(), EGARCH(lev).fit()]
print(round(fits[1].gamma[0], 3), round(fits[2].gamma[0], 3))
table = fits[0].compare(*fits[1:], criterion="bic")
print([row[0] for row in table.rows])
print(fits[2].unconditional_variance, round(fits[2].persistence, 3))

0.073 -0.057
['EGARCH(1, 1, 1)', 'GJR(1, 1, 1)', 'GARCH(1, 1)']
None 0.966

For EGARCH, persistence is \(\sum_j \beta_j\) (the AR root of the log variance) and unconditional_variance is None: the stationary level is the mean of a lognormal and has no closed form in the reported parameters. For the level families persistence is \(\sum\alpha_i + \tfrac12\sum\gamma_k + \sum\beta_j\).

ARMA means: ARGARCH to ARMAFIGARCH#

\[y_t = c + \sum_{i=1}^{p} \phi_i y_{t-i} + \varepsilon_t + \sum_{j=1}^{q} \theta_j \varepsilon_{t-j}, \qquad \varepsilon_t = \sigma_t z_t.\]

The AR*/ARMA* fronts take ar_lags and ma_lags (both default to 1 on the ARMA* fronts) and return ARMAGARCHResult or ARMAFIGARCHResult, which add mean_order, ma_params, the mean-side stability / is_stationary and invertibility / is_invertible verdicts, and is_structurally_constrained. const is the recursion intercept \(c\), as in ARResult.

from cultivars.univariate.conditional_variance import ARGARCH, ARMAGARCH

argarch = ARGARCH(r, ar_lags=2).fit()
plain = GARCH(r, ar_lags=2).fit()
print(argarch.nobs, argarch.mean_order, round(argarch.llf - plain.llf, 8))
print(argarch.is_stationary, argarch.is_structurally_constrained)

armagarch = ARMAGARCH(r, ar_lags=1, ma_lags=1).fit()
print(list(armagarch.params))

1998 (2, 0) 0.0
True False
['const', 'ar.L1', 'ma.L1', 'omega', 'alpha[1]', 'beta[1]']

ARGARCH(y, ar_lags=k) and GARCH(y, ar_lags=k) are the same estimator; the first returns the richer record. With a moving-average block the mean is searched inside the stationary-invertible region, so is_stationary then verifies the transform rather than describing the data (is_structurally_constrained is True).

Long memory: FIGARCH#

\[\sigma_t^2 = \frac{\omega}{1 - \beta} + \Bigl[1 - \frac{(1 - \phi L)(1 - L)^d}{1 - \beta L}\Bigr] \varepsilon_t^2.\]

The order is fixed at (1, d, 1); the parameters are omega, phi, d and beta, each of phi, d, beta inside \((0, 1)\). The ARCH(\(\infty\)) weights sum to one for every \(d > 0\), so FIGARCHResult reports persistence = 1.0, is_covariance_stationary = False and half_life = inf, has no unconditional_variance, and refuses simulate.

from cultivars.univariate.conditional_variance import FIGARCH

fig = FIGARCH(r[:1000], truncation=200).fit()
print(list(fig.params), fig.truncation)
print(fig.persistence, fig.is_covariance_stationary, fig.half_life)

['const', 'omega', 'phi', 'd', 'beta'] 200
1.0 False inf

Innovation distributions#

The conditional-variance models are Gaussian only: there is no dist argument, and the reported llf is the Gaussian likelihood. With fat-tailed data, read the estimates as Gaussian quasi-maximum likelihood (consistent for the variance parameters under correct mean and variance specification) and inspect standardized_resid for the tail shape. simulate also draws Gaussian \(z_t\).

Heavy tails and leverage are available on the latent side: SV(y, dist="t") makes \(\varepsilon_t\) Student-t with estimated degrees of freedom, and SV(y, leverage=True) correlates the return and log-variance innovations. The two cannot be combined.

Persistence and stationarity#

Stationarity on the variance side is persistence < 1 alone (is_covariance_stationary), and half_life is \(\log 0.5 / \log(\text{persistence})\). The constant-mean results carry no mean-stationarity verdict; the ARMA* results report it separately through is_stationary.

The level families’ estimator rejects any draw with persistence at or above 0.999, so an integrated or explosive GARCH comes back just below the bound and is_covariance_stationary is True for every GARCH and GJR fit. Read a persistence above about 0.99 as “integrated”, not as a finite half-life to trust.

rng = np.random.default_rng(7)
h, x = np.ones(1500), np.zeros(1500)
for t in range(1, 1500):
    h[t] = 0.01 + 0.2 * x[t - 1] ** 2 + 0.82 * h[t - 1]   # true sum 1.02
    x[t] = np.sqrt(h[t]) * rng.standard_normal()
explosive = GARCH(x, mean="zero").fit()
print(round(explosive.persistence, 3), explosive.is_covariance_stationary)

0.995 True

Stochastic volatility: SV#

\[y_t = c + \exp(h_t / 2)\,\varepsilon_t, \qquad h_{t+1} = \mu + \phi\,(h_t - \mu) + \sigma\,\eta_t, \qquad \varepsilon_t, \eta_t \sim N(0, 1)\]

(Taylor, 1986). The likelihood is an integral over the latent path, and SV offers four ways to do it:

from cultivars.univariate.stochastic_volatility import SV

rng = np.random.default_rng(11)
m = 300
lv = np.empty(m)
lv[0] = -1.0
for t in range(1, m):
    lv[t] = -1.0 + 0.95 * (lv[t - 1] + 1.0) + np.sqrt(0.05) * rng.standard_normal()
ret = np.exp(lv / 2) * rng.standard_normal(m)

sv = SV(ret, mean="zero").fit()
print(sv.method, sv.nobs, sv.n_params)
print(round(sv.mu, 3), round(sv.phi, 3), round(sv.sigma2, 4))
print(round(sv.unconditional_variance, 3), round(sv.half_life, 1))
print(sv.forecast_variance(3).round(3))
lower, upper = sv.volatility_bands(alpha=0.10)
print(bool(np.all(lower < sv.volatility) and np.all(sv.volatility < upper)))

SVResult carries mean, mu, phi, sigma2, nu (None without dist="t"), rho (0.0 without leverage), method, the smoothed log_variance and log_variance_std, and volatility (\(e^{\hat h_t/2}\)). volatility_bands(alpha=0.05) exponentiates normal bands on the smoothed log variance. forecast_variance(steps) propagates the last smoothed log-variance moments through the AR(1) with the log-normal correction and converges to unconditional_variance. state_space is the fitted model as a NonlinearSSM (read by the particle filter); quasi_state_space is the linearized LinearGaussianSSM. to_pandas columns are observed, log_variance, volatility, volatility_lower, volatility_upper and standardized.

The Gibbs posterior:

post = SV(ret, mean="zero").sample(n_draws=250, n_burn=50, seed=0)
print(post.method, post.n_kept, post.phi_draws.shape)

0.94 (3, 300)

n_draws counts all iterations, burn-in included, so n_kept = (n_draws - n_burn) / thin. SVPosterior exposes mu_draws, phi_draws, sigma2_draws, mean_draws, h_draws ((S, n) under Gibbs), log_variance and volatility (posterior means), volatility_quantiles(levels), convergence(), posterior_replications() and simulate(n, draw=None); the prior is VolatilityPrior, passed as sample(prior=...). Convergence and predictive checks are covered in The Bayesian workflow.

Trend with stochastic volatility: UCSV#

\[y_t = \tau_t + e^{h_t/2}\varepsilon_t, \qquad \tau_{t+1} = \tau_t + e^{q_t/2}\eta_t,\]

with \(h_t\) and \(q_t\) random walks of standard deviation \(\gamma\) (Stock and Watson, 2007). UCSV(y, gamma=0.2) holds \(\gamma\) at the Stock-Watson value; gamma=None estimates both vol-of-vols under RandomWalkVolatilityPrior. fit() runs the Gibbs sampler and returns a UCSVPosterior.

from cultivars.univariate.stochastic_volatility import UCSV

rng = np.random.default_rng(0)
infl = np.cumsum(0.2 * rng.standard_normal(150)) + 0.7 * rng.standard_normal(150)
ucsv = UCSV(infl).fit(n_draws=200, n_burn=50, seed=0)
print(ucsv.n_kept, ucsv.gamma_fixed, ucsv.trend.shape)
paths = ucsv.forecast(4, n_paths=500, seed=0)
print(paths.shape)
print(list(ucsv.to_pandas().columns))

Variance forecasts and VaR-style use#

What exists:

  • SVResult.forecast_variance(steps): expected variance \(E[e^{h_{T+k}} \mid y]\) for \(k = 1, \ldots, steps\).

  • UCSVPosterior.forecast(steps, ...): predictive draws of the series.

  • Nothing on the GARCH-type results. GARCHResult, FIGARCHResult and the ARMA variants have no forecast or forecast_variance method, and SVPosterior has none either.

For a GARCH(1, 1) the forecasts follow from the fitted recursion: the one-step variance is \(\sigma^2_{T+1} = \omega + \alpha\varepsilon_T^2 + \beta\sigma_T^2\), and for \(h > 1\), \(E[\sigma^2_{T+h}] = \bar\sigma^2 + \pi^{h-1}(\sigma^2_{T+1} - \bar\sigma^2)\) with \(\pi\) the persistence and \(\bar\sigma^2\) the unconditional variance.

s2_next = (garch.omega + garch.alpha[0] * garch.resid[-1] ** 2
           + garch.beta[0] * garch.conditional_variance[-1])
ubar, pi = garch.unconditional_variance, garch.persistence
path = ubar + pi ** np.arange(5) * (s2_next - ubar)
print(round(s2_next, 3), path.round(3))

1.005 [1.005 1.033 1.06  1.087 1.114]

A one-step value-at-risk is a quantile of the predictive distribution, \(\hat c + \sigma_{T+1} q_\alpha\). Under the Gaussian likelihood \(q_\alpha\) is the normal quantile; the empirical quantile of standardized_resid (filtered historical simulation) is the usual remedy for fat tails. Quantile forecasts are scored with pinball_loss(); here the in-sample 1% quantiles of the GARCH fit beat a constant-variance quantile:

from scipy.stats import norm
from cultivars.forecast.scoring import pinball_loss

z01 = norm.ppf(0.01)
var_gauss = garch.const + np.sqrt(s2_next) * z01
var_fhs = garch.const + np.sqrt(s2_next) * np.quantile(garch.standardized_resid, 0.01)
print(round(var_gauss, 3), round(var_fhs, 3))

q_garch = garch.fittedvalues + garch.conditional_volatility * z01
q_const = np.full(r.size, r.mean() + r.std() * z01)
print(round(pinball_loss(r, q_garch, 0.01).mean(), 4),
      round(pinball_loss(r, q_const, 0.01).mean(), 4))

-2.285 -2.36
0.0427 0.0509

Out-of-sample evaluation over rolling origins is Backtest; because the GARCH results have no forecast, pass a predict= hook built from the recursion above. See Forecasting and forecast evaluation.

Simulating from a fit#

GARCHResult.simulate(n=200, *, seed=None, burn=...) runs the family’s own recursion from the unconditional variance with Gaussian shocks and applies the mean on top; ARMAGARCHResult drives the full ARMA mean. SVResult.simulate and SVPosterior.simulate start the log variance from its stationary law. FIGARCHResult.simulate (and the ARMA variant) always raises SpecificationError, because no finite burn-in makes a long-memory variance forget its start.

from cultivars.exceptions import SpecificationError

sim = garch.simulate(20000, seed=0)
print(sim.shape, round(sim.var(), 2))
print(sv.simulate(5, seed=0).round(3))
try:
    fig.simulate(10)
except SpecificationError as err:
    print(str(err)[:40])

How the pieces connect#

  • Mean first, jointly. A GARCH error on an ARMA mean is fitted in one likelihood by the ARMA* fronts; there is no two-step “fit ARIMA, then GARCH on its residuals” API, and ARMAGARCHResult is the joint analogue of ARMAResult (Univariate linear models).

  • Long memory. FIGARCH is the variance counterpart of ARFIMA; the latent counterpart is LongMemorySV, estimated by Whittle in the frequency domain.

  • Decomposition. UCSV relaxes the constant variances of UnobservedComponents with trend="level".

  • State space. SVResult.state_space is a NonlinearSSM whose particle_filter estimates the exact likelihood or reads new data with the fitted law (State-space models and filtering).

  • Bayesian tools. SVPosterior and UCSVPosterior provide convergence(); SVPosterior also provides posterior_replications for predictive checks (The Bayesian workflow).

  • Multivariate volatility. Stochastic volatility in VAR innovations (BVARSV), factor stochastic volatility (FactorSV) and time-varying-parameter VARs with SV are on Bayesian VARs, Large and high-dimensional systems and Nonlinear, time-varying and regime-switching VARs; identification through heteroskedasticity (StochasticVolatilitySVAR) is on Structural identification.

  • Forecast evaluation. Quantile and density forecasts built from these results are scored by cultivars.forecast.scoring and cultivars.forecast.calibration (Forecasting and forecast evaluation).

Nuances and gotchas#

  • No variance forecast on GARCH-type results. Compute it from omega, alpha, gamma, beta, the last resid and the last conditional_variance as shown above. SVResult is the only record with forecast_variance.

  • No standard errors. Every summary says “Standard errors are not yet available for this estimator.”; use likelihood_ratio_test between nested fits (for example GARCH inside GJR) for inference.

  • Gaussian innovations only in the GARCH family. No Student-t or skewed-t option; the likelihood is Gaussian QML.

  • Persistence is capped below 0.999 for GARCH and GJR (and \(|\sum\beta_j| < 0.999\) for EGARCH), so is_covariance_stationary cannot be False for those fits even on explosive data. The GARCH docstring states otherwise; trust the behaviour shown above.

  • EGARCH and GJR sign conventions differ. Leverage is gamma > 0 in GJR and gamma < 0 in EGARCH. GJR persistence counts gamma at half weight.

  • Effective sample. nobs is \(n - \text{ar\_lags}\) for any front with an AR mean (GARCH(y, ar_lags=2) has nobs = n - 2) and \(n\) otherwise; conditional_variance, resid and fittedvalues are aligned with the last nobs observations, and compare refuses to rank fits with different nobs.

    from cultivars.exceptions import DimensionError
try:
    garch.compare(plain)
except DimensionError as err:
    print(err)

    information criteria are only comparable across a common sample; got differing observation counts [1998, 2000].

  • The first conditional variance is a backcast, not unconditional_variance; conditional_variance[0] comes from an exponentially weighted mean square of the residuals.

  • FIGARCH. The order is fixed at (1, d, 1); has_long_memory is True for nearly every fit because d cannot reach zero; half_life is inf and simulate raises. The ARMA mean is ARMAFIGARCH; FIGARCH itself accepts no ar_lags.

  • SV: the QML likelihood is not the SV likelihood. Under method="qml" llf is the Gaussian likelihood of the linearized model; information criteria compare only within one method (the comparison label includes the method). QML cannot identify leverage (SV(y, leverage=True).fit() returns rho = 0.0) and identifies nu weakly (on Gaussian data it drifted to about 500); use fit(method="particle") or sample() for either.

  • SV sizes and cost. SV needs at least 50 observations and UCSV at least 40. QML runs a generic Kalman filter inside a numerical-gradient optimizer and takes several seconds on a few hundred observations; the particle methods are much slower.

  • Sampler bookkeeping. n_draws includes n_burn; sample(n_draws=100, n_burn=200) raises. UCSV.fit is a sampler (its signature is fit(n_draws=3000, n_burn=1000, thin=1, prior=None, seed=None)), and its result has no llf or information criteria.

  • LongMemorySV lives in a multivariate module. Import it from cultivars.multivariate.structural.stochastic_volatility, even though it is a single-series model.
